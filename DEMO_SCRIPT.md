# Demo Script — Supply Chain Reorder Alert Agent
**Target runtime: ~5 minutes**

---

## 0 · Before you start (setup, not spoken)

- `docker compose up --build` is running (Python 3.12 image); seeder has completed
- Browser open at **http://localhost:8501**
- Simulator is **running** (▶ Start pressed in sidebar — Status shows 🟢 Running)
- Drain Speed slider is at **1× Normal** for a measured start
- Atlas UI open in a second tab (optional — for showing indexes live)
- **Watch for these SKUs** — they have a seeded rising demand trend and will produce MEDIUM confidence even with stock above 5 days:
  - `MED-3017` (Insulin Glargine · DC-Texas · pharmaceutical)
  - `SURG-0084` (Nitrile Gloves · DC-Ohio · surgical)
  - `DIAG-0331` (COVID/Flu Test Kit · DC-Ohio · diagnostic)

## 1 · Set the scene `[0:00 – 0:30]`

> "Healthcare distribution centres manage hundreds of SKUs across multiple locations.
> When a product runs low, someone has to notice, find the right supplier, calculate
> the right quantity, and place the order — manually, often under pressure.
> This demo shows an AI agent doing all of that automatically, in real time,
> using MongoDB Atlas as the backbone."

**👉 Point at the KPI row (five cards across the top).**

> "Five live KPIs: SKUs below reorder point, active alerts pending,
> orders awaiting approval, auto-approved count, and escalated alerts.
> All recompute on every refresh."

**👉 Point at the three-panel layout below.**

> "Three panels: live inventory grid on the left — critical items sort to the top
> automatically. Active alerts in the middle, agent decisions on the right.
> Everything refreshes every 10 seconds via MongoDB Change Streams."

---

## 2 · Watch an alert arrive `[0:30 – 1:30]`

**👉 Point at the left panel — find a red 🔴 CRITICAL card near the top.**

> "Critical items sort to the top automatically. The progress bar shows
> how far below the reorder point we are — this one is nearly empty.
> Each card shows On Hand, On Order, Reorder Point, and Effective stock."

**👉 Point at the Active Alerts section when a new alert card appears.**

> "The simulator writes a reorder alert to MongoDB every 10 seconds,
> mimicking what Atlas Stream Processing would do in production off a
> Kafka feed. The dashboard picks it up via a Change Stream — no polling."

> "Notice the urgency badge: CRITICAL means less than 2 days of stock.
> The time-ago label tells us exactly when this alert fired."

**👉 Point at the `PROCESSING…` badge on the alert card.**

> "The agent is already working. It watches the same collection via
> Change Stream and triggers the moment a new alert lands."

---

## 3 · Show the agent decision `[1:30 – 3:00]`

**👉 Wait for the order card to appear in the Agent Decisions section, then walk through it.**

> "The agent ran a multi-agent LangGraph pipeline. Rather than one big LLM call,
> three specialist agents each do a scoped job:
> a ReAct retrieval agent decides which MongoDB queries to run — supplier list,
> consumption trend, Atlas Search, Vector Search — and only fetches what it needs.
> An analysis agent evaluates the options and assigns confidence.
> A recommendation agent calculates quantity and writes the rationale.
> An audit agent validates the output before anything is persisted.
> If validation fails, the pipeline retries automatically — up to twice —
> before escalating to the human queue."

**👉 Point at the confidence and status badges.**

> "Confidence has four strict criteria — all four must be true simultaneously
> for HIGH: at least 5 days of stock remaining, a stable or decreasing
> consumption trend, a confirmed supplier with a 95%+ fill rate via Atlas
> Search, and at least 14 days of consumption history.
> LOW is triggered by any single red flag: under 2 days of stock,
> a rising trend, a fill rate below 85%, or thin history."
>
> "If confidence is HIGH *and* the total cost is under $2,500, both
> conditions of the auto-approve policy are met, so the order is approved
> instantly." *(point at the ⚡ AUTO badge)*
>
> "Orders at or above $2,500 are always held for human review,
> regardless of confidence — finance keeps visibility over larger spend."

**👉 If the alert is for MED-3017, SURG-0084, or DIAG-0331 — call this out explicitly.**

> "This one will be MEDIUM confidence even though there are more than 5 days
> of stock. That's because the 14-day consumption trend is *increasing* —
> demand has been doubling over the last 30 days. The trend is a genuine
> gate: the agent won't self-approve when demand is accelerating, because
> the reorder quantity calculated today may already be too small by delivery day."

**👉 Expand `💬 Rationale / 🧠 Vector Search`.**

> "Plain-English rationale generated by GPT-5.4, ready for a non-technical
> approver to read. It references days of stock, consumption trend,
> supplier fill rate, and cost."

> "Below the rationale you'll see the Vector Search results. The agent
> embedded the current situation — SKU, location, urgency, trend — with
> Voyage AI and retrieved the most semantically similar historical orders
> *from the same product category*, using Atlas Vector Search pre-filtering
> so cross-category noise is eliminated before scoring.
> Similarity scores above 0.78. These outcomes feed directly into the
> LLM prompt as precedent."

---

## 4 · Show memory growing + reject / escalation flow `[3:00 – 4:00]`

**👉 Point at the sidebar Demo Status panel.**

> "Long-term Memories starts at zero. After every decision the agent writes
> a natural-language summary, embeds it, and stores it in `agent_memory`.
> From the second alert for the same SKU onwards, the agent retrieves its
> own past learnings via Vector Search and factors them into the next order."

**👉 Find a `MEDIUM` confidence order with the AWAITING badge and click ❌ Reject.**

> "If a human rejects an order, two things happen simultaneously.
> First — the alert resets to pending and the Change Stream fires again.
> The agent re-processes it right now, this time with a ⚠ HUMAN REJECTED
> tag in short-term memory. The system prompt requires the agent to change
> its approach — different supplier, adjusted quantity — and explain what
> changed in the rationale."
>
> "Second — the rejection is written to long-term memory as an embedded
> document. Human oversight becomes a persistent training signal,
> not just a one-time gate."

**👉 Watch the new order appear and point at the rationale — it should reference the previous rejection.**

> "The rationale now explicitly acknowledges the prior rejection and
> explains the change."

**👉 Mention the escalation safety net — no need to demonstrate live unless time allows.**

> "There's a safety net built in. If the same alert is rejected three times,
> the agent stops trying and escalates automatically — it writes the alert to
> an escalation queue, marks the status 'escalated', and optionally fires a
> webhook to notify the operations team. Those alerts surface in a dedicated
> section on the dashboard so nothing falls through the cracks."

**👉 Now click ✅ Approve on any order.**

> "An approval is recorded the same way — ✓ HUMAN APPROVED in short-term
> memory, and an embedded entry in long-term memory. The agent will
> preferentially reuse that supplier for future alerts on this SKU."

---

## 5 · MongoDB Atlas features — rapid fire `[4:00 – 4:45]`

**👉 Point at sidebar Simulator Controls — show ▶ Start / ⏸ Pause / ⏹ Stop.**

> "The simulator is controlled live from the sidebar. Pausing it freezes
> inventory drain so you can inspect the current state without new alerts
> firing."

**👉 Move the ⚡ Drain Speed slider from 1× Normal up to 5× Demo or 10× Chaos.**

> "10× Chaos fires alerts across every SKU within seconds. Great for showing
> the agent handling a surge of concurrent alerts live."

**👉 Scroll down to Supplier Search in the sidebar, type `cold chain insulin`, press 🔍 Search.**

> "Atlas Search with a compound operator — supplier name matches score three
> times higher than capability notes, so exact name hits always outrank fuzzy
> notes hits. Fuzzy matching handles typos within one edit distance."

**👉 Briefly point at the Confidence Calibration expander in the main panel.**

> "As the agent processes more alerts, a calibration table fills in — predicted
> confidence level versus actual outcome. Over time you can see whether HIGH
> confidence correlates with resolved stock, or whether there are systematic
> gaps to tune."

> "Under the hood this demo touches: **Change Streams** with resume-token
> persistence; **Time Series collections** for 90 days of consumption history;
> **Atlas Search** with boosted compound scoring; **Atlas Vector Search** on
> two collections — order history pre-filtered by product category, and the
> agent's own long-term memory; a **TTL index** that auto-expires short-term
> memory after 24 hours; and **LangGraph's `MongoDBSaver`** checkpointing
> every graph state transition to MongoDB."

**👉 Scroll to the bottom of the sidebar — show the 🔄 Reset Demo button.**

> "One click clears all alerts, orders, agent memory, and checkpoints —
> inventory and suppliers stay intact. The simulator resets to 1× speed and
> resumes immediately. No reseeding, no container restart."

---

## 6 · Close `[4:45 – 5:00]`

> "In production, the simulator is replaced by an Atlas Stream Processing
> pipeline off Kafka. The agent code doesn't change — it only sees the
> `reorder_alerts` collection. MongoDB Atlas is the single platform
> handling storage, search, vector search, streaming, and agent memory —
> with the resilience and index coverage to keep every piece of it fast
> and reliable under load."
